# Before You See My Work...

...I'd like you to know the person who created it. My name is *Yuvraj Singh Sankhala*. I am an artist from Bikaner, Rajasthan. But if this page only tells you where I come from or what mediums I work with, then we've missed the most important part of meeting each other. Because art, to me, has never been about paint. It has always been about people. It has always been about the quiet moments that somehow change us forever.

There was a time in my life when everything felt as though it was slipping beyond my reach. The world kept moving, yet I felt strangely disconnected from it, as if I no longer had the privilege of deciding what my own life should look like. Then one evening, I sat down with a blank sheet of paper. Nothing existed on it. There were no expectations. No opinions. No rules. Only possibility. For the first time in a long time, I could create something that belonged entirely to me. Every line answered only to my hand. Every shadow existed because I wanted it to. Every mistake could become something beautiful. I didn't know it then, but I wasn't just learning how to draw. I was learning how to breathe again. Even today, whenever life feels louder than my own thoughts, I find myself returning to a canvas. Sometimes I paint until sunrise. Sometimes I forget that the world outside my room still exists. The painting doesn't judge me. It doesn't ask me to become someone else. It simply lets me exist. And somehow... that's enough.

People often compliment my drawings. I appreciate that. But I don't think drawing is my greatest gift. I think my greatest gift is noticing. I notice things most people walk past. The smell of rain before the first drop touches the earth. The warmth drifting from a bakery before I even see it. Freshly washed hair moving with the wind. Dust dancing through afternoon sunlight. The silence that follows thunder. The way old buildings carry memories long after the people who built them are gone. The kindness hidden inside tired eyes. The smile someone gives while quietly carrying a burden no one else knows about. I have always believed that the world is speaking. Very softly. You only have to stop long enough to hear it. Maybe that's why I paint. Because some moments deserve to exist a little longer than time allows.

One of the paintings closest to my heart portrays a woman living with cancer. Not because of her illness. But because of her strength. She continues smiling. She continues comforting others. She continues carrying hope while quietly carrying pain. I painted her because the strongest people I've ever known rarely looked strong. They simply kept choosing love over surrender. Another painting I treasure is a portrait of my mother holding me. If everything else disappeared tomorrow, I think I'd still search for that one first. Not because it's my finest work. But because it reminds me that before I was ever an artist... I was someone's child.

People often ask me what my paintings mean. I don't think they're asking the right question.The better question is— "What did it make you feel?" Because I don't believe art exists to be understood. I believe it exists to communicate. Animals don't need words to understand one another. Neither do we. Music speaks. Stories speak. Architecture speaks. Silence speaks. Art speaks. If one of my paintings reminds you of your grandmother... Or a place you haven't visited in years. Or someone you've lost. If it makes you smile. If it makes you uncomfortable. If it makes you stop scrolling through life for just a few moments... Then we've already had a conversation. Even if we've never shaken hands.
